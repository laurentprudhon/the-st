Blitter — four sheets, one chip

C101643, U101 on the 1040STE: the blocks, one word, the register seam, and the bus window.
Educational schematics, drawn from the recovered Atari designs (TA-4021, ST-4051, ST-4082), Atari's BLiTTER manual of 25 January 1990, the board's net list and the Hatari model. Functional, not to scale, and not a floorplan — no die photograph of this part exists, so no layout is claimed anywhere.

00 · The chip as blocks

Bus side, address path, datapath, storage and control — and the five things deliberately absent.

01 · One word, end to end

Read, shift, merge, mask, write: the eight steps, the read-count table (4, 8 or 12 clocks a word), the 32-bit source buffer, and a masked line worked by hand.

02 · The register seam

Sixty-two bytes at $FF8A00: sixteen halftone words, the pointer registers, three end masks, and one word of control exploded bit by bit.

03 · The bus window

Four clocks of CPU, four of arbitration, 64 accesses of four clocks — the 63/64 conflict, the frame budget, and the one wire that leaves the chip.

Four SVGs, one generator: python3 build_schematics.py && python3 check_overlaps.py. Labels are self-audited on every build (0.60 em per character against the box), and both passes print zero on a clean build. Provenance per sheet; the study is ../blitter-implementation.md.
